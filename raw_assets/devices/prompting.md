# Devices — what the wording did

Read before the next device sheet. Briefs: `.tmp/gen/briefs/dev_*.txt`; the frames' records hold each prompt sent.

- **The anchor is `cdev_b-v1`** (a plain `gen`), every later sheet (`cdev_a`, `cdev_c` to `cdev_f`) an `edit` with a
  style picture of four of its variants (`any_bench-v3`, `any_cot-v3`, `any_lifesupport-v3`, `wat_melter-v3`). The
  `dev_*` sheets before it drew heavy plant, grimy; the owner asked for compact, kept equipment (GAM-12).
- **What made it compact and kept** (v3, score 4): "one small crew capsule of a space station… one operator's work
  station and living quarters, so every machine is a compact, self-contained, automated unit sized for a small cabin
  and run by one person, its works boxed in behind bolted panels — not heavy plant: no excavator arms, no drill rigs,
  no chimneys, no wheelbarrows", and "lived in and kept: no grime, no rust, no oil stains, no soot, no litter". A
  device that digs (drill, scoop, tether) was described as a sealed intake unit at a hull port, "no drill bit, no
  rig". Living touches asked by name (a photo by the cot, a toothbrush cup) came out.
- **"Tilted a little from straight down" came out three-quarter** (v1, about 35–45°, front faces large); the owner
  called it too much. What worked (v2): "tilted only about 15 to 20 degrees from straight down, as in a classic
  top-down management game; the top surfaces fill most of the sprite; only a narrow strip of its front face shows
  along its bottom edge; no side faces".
- **A side face is hard to get from an edit** (`sdev_b`, trial): with the devices' style picture as image 1, "standing
  a little to the left… a narrower strip of its left side face" changed nothing; v2, "Unlike image 1, whose objects
  show no side faces at all, every object here must clearly show its left side face", gave a faint one. The style
  picture pins the camera: a new camera wants a plain `gen` anchor first.
- **Upright cabinets turn to a front view** unless described by their top (`dev_c` v3 failed with "the top surface
  or lid is its largest visible face"). `cdev_c` fixed it: "every object is low, so its top is its largest visible
  face", and each cabinet as what its top is — "its top is a frosted blue-green glass lid over rows of small seed
  drawers", "a low under-counter dishwasher whose top is a steel worktop with a small sink".
- **No bolts, and the door as parts** (`kit_d` caps, `kit_e` panels, `doors_d` door parts, score 4, in use; the
  owner: "Lets not use bolted plates because they still add blockiness", "Are doors not build as walls? From parts?").
  Since the walls lean out from the room (the page's 3D walls), the front panel and the face-on door serve every side;
  the side panels, the trim panel and the edge-on door are unused.
  "Smooth, heavy, continuous surfaces: NO bolts, NO rivets, NO screw heads anywhere" held on every sheet; caps take "a
  smooth rounded grey steel edge trim with no bolts". The door is cut-outs laid on the kit: "Each asset is only the
  part itself, with transparent background all round it: no wall, no panel, no backing plate behind it" — the door
  face on and edge on, shut and open, the lintel both ways, a control box (unused). A door housing taller than the
  walls (`doors_c`, unused) was tried first and replaced by parts.
- **The kit drawn again to join as one** (superseded) (`kit_b` caps, `kit_c` panels and doors, score 4, in use; the owner: "Kit
  works, but we need better images… make pattern less repeatable"): "one long continuous… cap plate running along
  each wall, as if cast in one piece", each continuing edge with "no rim, no bolt, no seam, no bevel and no line at
  all", and a seam variant the page lays one cell in three; panels "one long continuous face along each wall", their
  joining edges plain. The door "filling the panel's full height and about half its width… a thick oval frame…
  standing proud of the wall, its inner jamb faces in deep shadow" came out deep and large; its frame overhangs the
  panel by a fifth. Ask distinct marks away: "with no single distinctive mark that would show when a panel repeats".
- **Walls are a kit** (`kit_a`, score 4, superseded; style picture of a wall top, a face, the watertight door and a
  lintel). Caps by which edges continue: "Along every edge where the wall ends, a raised bolted grey steel rim; every
  edge where the wall continues into the next square has no rim and is plain" — post, end, straight, corner, tee,
  cross, each named by its open edges, all came out right; the page turns them. "Nothing stands or lies on any cap."
  Panels "three times as wide as tall" came out about 2.35:1 and the side panels 1:2.7, so the page's wall height is
  the panel's own (.43 of a cell; since raised to .5, the panels stretched, so the doors could grow within the face;
  the wall now stands three panels high, the fitting on the top one, and the door stretches to its height). The door asked "set deep into a thick hazard-striped oval frame: the frame's inner
  jamb faces show in shadow… Clearly a heavy three-dimensional door, not a picture of one" has a recess but is small
  in its panel; the open leaf "swung out toward the viewer… its thick edge showing" overhangs the panel's foot.
  Everything below this line was superseded by the kit.
- **A wall block is drawn as two flat pieces, not one** (GAM-10). As one tilted block (`blocks_a`, unused) the model
  foreshortens its top however asked: a third of the height, then 55% ("a full square as deep as it is wide"), then
  overshot to a face twice the top (portrait cells, "an exact square"). Drawn flat it gets them right (`walls_b`,
  score 4): "rows 1 and 2 are squares seen straight down, rows 3 and 4 are wide strips seen straight on", the tops
  "filling its square edge to edge… edges plain so tops laid side by side join seamlessly", the faces "a strip one
  metre wide and a third of a metre tall". The page stacks a top over a face.
- **Doors are submarine watertight doors** (`doors_a`, score 5, in use; the owner: "more explicit door like on
  submarines"): "an oval grey steel door leaf with a large central hand wheel and six locking dogs round its rim, in
  a thick oval frame ringed with black and yellow hazard stripes, heavy hinges on one side", its faces in use. Its door tile
  "drawn flat and face on… filling the square" put the door on the wall's roof (the owner: "Door on the roof is not
  correct"), so it is kept unused; from above a door shows its lintel (`doors_b`, score 5): "A thick grey steel
  lintel beam with black and yellow hazard stripes runs across the square… a small green lamp… No door is drawn: only
  the wall's top over it", amber when open. A side wall's door shows on its west face as "a tall narrow strip… the
  watertight door's face seen almost edge-on". "Every wheel, handle and valve is
  grey steel; red appears nowhere" kept the wheels grey. The face strips came out about 2:1, not the 3:1 asked; the
  page squashes them a little.
- **Doors apart from walls** (`walls_b`, superseded): "The doors stand out from the walls at a glance: black and yellow hazard-striped
  frames, a grey steel slab, a small green lamp". The door tops came out narrower than square. Set into a wall top
  at their own proportion, wall showed across the passage, so the page fills the cell with the door, and a side
  wall's door is the front door's top turned (`any_door_top_side_*` came out too narrow to fill a cell, unused).
- **Things on wall tops** (`walls_c`, with a style picture of four `walls_b` tops): "On each top sits or runs one
  thing, also seen straight down and kept inside the square". Say "grey" for every fitting with a wheel or handle: the
  elbow's valve wheel came out red unasked, and a `--mask` redo of it drew a fragment of plate.
- **A 4×4 sheet with drawings taller than their cells** cuts with `qq sprites cut --grid=4x4 --whole=0.6`: at the
  default 0.85 the door tops were split across a row line.
- **Footprint in cells reads**: "2 cells wide by 1 deep", "2 by 2", "3 wide by 2 deep" gave the proportions asked.
- **A 3×3 grid with tall drawings touches across rows**: cut with `qq sprites cut --grid=3x3`.
- **Mixed sizes on one 4×4 sheet work** (`dev_f`): 3×2 machines and 1-cell wall pieces at one scale.
